# Foghorn Alley Brewing: a taproom brewery that also sells wholesale

This describes one business, its past and its year. Facts marked † come from the real San Francisco brewery's public Regulation Crowdfunding filing that this business is modeled on (see `SOURCES.md`); a † vouches for the fact it follows, not for the date or reason the story gives it. The company, its people and its account numbers are invented.

## 1. The business and the time frame

- **Business:** Foghorn Alley Brewing LLC, a small brewery with a taproom in a converted warehouse in San Francisco's SoMa district, organized in 2014†. It brews about a thousand barrels a year and makes money three ways: beer, food and events in its own taproom; kegs and bottles sold wholesale to bars, restaurants and bottle shops that it delivers itself; and a little online merchandise.
- **Size:** 31 employees (the real brewery's count in 2024†), most of them part-time taproom staff paid an hourly wage plus tips. Revenue was about `$1,026,000` in 2022† and `$1,173,000` in 2023†, counting `$122,770` of tips† that pass through payroll. It loses money: about `$219,000` in 2022† and `$225,000` in 2023†.
- **Time range:** 2023-01-01 to 2023-12-31, currency USD.
- **Where the 2023 money comes from (for scale)†:** taproom beer `$557,839`, taproom food `$40,299`, private events `$41,789`, service charges `$24,111`, the San Francisco health-care surcharge added to every check `$20,687`, wholesale bottles `$199,360`, wholesale kegs `$162,756`, merchandise `$11,818`, online store `$3,059`, less discounts and comps of `$19,061`.
- **Where it goes (for scale)†:** rent `$145,830` a year, payroll about `$490,000` including tips, utilities `$54,449`, trash `$11,537`, insurance `$32,261`, card processing fees `$22,423`, advertising `$46,799`, accounting `$19,494`, cleaning `$14,300`, equipment rental `$16,677`, interest `$42,520`, and `$56,488` of guaranteed payments to the owners.

## 2. History before the start

- Two co-founders opened the brewery in 2014† with money from about twenty outside investors who hold small LLC stakes. The taproom build-out and brewhouse were paid for long ago; the equipment is mostly depreciated.
- In June 2020 the brewery sold `$100,000` of community bonds through an online small-business bond platform at 9% interest, maturing June 2025†. The platform pulls one fixed monthly payment from the operating account, pays the bondholders, and charges a `$25` processing fee each month†.
- In January 2022 it took a `$650,000` SBA Economic Injury Disaster Loan at 3.75% for 30 years†. Payments are still deferred through 2023, so interest builds up but no money moves.
- Other debts carried into 2023: a large interest-free line of credit from an outside business partner, drawn since 2019†, a loan from one co-founder's mother† and a small one from the CEO†, a lender loan arranged through the brewery's accounting software (WebBank)†, and a small bank loan†.
- On 2023-01-01 the operating account holds about `$77,000`† in total across accounts. A leftover account from the 2020 Paycheck Protection Program loan still holds a couple of hundred dollars†. Wholesale customers owe about `$17,000`†. The December card statement is unpaid, December's sales tax is due, several hundred gift cards sold for the holidays are outstanding, and dozens of kegs sit at customers' bars on deposit.

## 3. Institutions (the only sources of data)

- **Bank of America:** the operating checking account (`Checking ••4410`), a savings account, the old PPP account, and one checking account nicknamed `DO NOT USE FOR CHECKS ••7092`† that keeps getting overdrawn. Shows each posted transaction with a running balance and a monthly statement. Checks clear days or weeks after they are written.
- **Square (point of sale)†:** taproom card and cash sales, tips, the service charge and health surcharge lines, comps and discounts, event deposits, and gift cards. Card sales are paid out to checking the next business day, net of processing fees. Cash goes in the safe and is deposited at the bank a few times a week, and the drawer is sometimes over or short at close†.
- **Chase business cards:** cards for the founders, the head brewer and the taproom manager, with a monthly statement paid from checking. Several card numbers have been closed and reissued over the years†, and more are this year, so the same spending shows up under different card numbers.
- **Shopify:** the online store for merchandise and a few beer pickups, with payouts to checking every few days.
- **Payroll provider:** semi-monthly payroll for about 31 people†. Each run debits checking for net pay, taxes and the state retirement program (CalSavers)†, and pays card tips out to the staff who earned them.
- **Lenders:** the bond platform (monthly ACH pull), the accounting-software lender (monthly ACH pulls), the small bank loan (monthly), and the SBA (no payments due in 2023).
- **Tax agencies:** California sales tax filed quarterly with prepayments, federal beer excise filed with the TTB, and California beer excise. Every bottle and can sold carries a California container deposit that the brewery collects and remits (the books carry a can-and-bottle-deposit liability†).

## 4. The cast

- **Theo Marchetti, co-founder and CEO.** Runs the money and the wholesale relationships. An optimist who juggles which bill to pay this week; lends the company small amounts from his own account when payroll is tight (the real filing shows loans from the CEO†) and pays himself last.
- **Dana Okafor, co-founder.** Runs the brewhouse with the head brewer and handles ingredient and packaging purchases on her Chase card. Careful, and annoyed when cards get reissued in the middle of an order.
- **Rafa Iturbe, head brewer.** Schedules brews and packaging runs, orders hops and malt, rents the CO2 and forklift equipment, and decides when a batch is not good enough to sell.
- **Jess Lin, taproom manager.** Runs the floor, closes out Square at night, counts the drawer, makes the bank deposit, books private events and sells gift cards. Writes the cash over/short on the closing sheet.
- **Marcus Bell, wholesale sales rep.** Takes orders from about 40 bars, restaurants and bottle shops, loads the van and delivers. Collects checks at delivery from some accounts and chases the others.
- **Taproom staff (about 20 bartenders and servers), a part-time kitchen crew, and a contract cleaning crew.**
- **Wholesale customers:** neighborhood bars that pay by check at delivery; restaurants on 30-day terms; a small bottle-shop chain that pays in 45-60 days; a grocery account that pays by ACH on its own schedule. Kegs go out with a deposit and come back empty, late, or never.
- **Event customers:** companies booking holiday parties and team offsites, and couples booking rehearsal dinners. They pay a deposit by card when booking and the balance after the event, plus a service charge.
- **Vendors:** the landlord (rent about `$12,150` a month by ACH), the electric and gas utility, the trash and recycling company, malt and hop suppliers (some on annual hop contracts), a can and label printer, a packaging supplier, a cold-storage warehouse for packaged beer, a CO2 and equipment rental company, an insurance broker, an outside accountant, and a contract cleaning company.
- **Owners and investors:** the two co-founders receive guaranteed payments when cash allows; the outside investors receive nothing.

## 5. Money flows

| Flow | How it moves |
|---|---|
| Taproom guests -> Square | card sales with tips, service charge and health surcharge; paid out to checking next business day, net of fees |
| Taproom cash -> bank | counted at close and deposited a few times a week; the deposit rarely matches the day exactly |
| Gift cards | sold through Square (cash in now), redeemed later as taproom sales |
| Event customers -> Square or ACH | a deposit at booking, the balance after the event |
| Wholesale customers -> checking | checks collected at delivery, checks mailed later, or ACH on 30-60 day terms; keg deposits charged and refunded |
| Online store -> Shopify -> checking | payouts every few days, net of fees |
| Payroll provider -> staff | semi-monthly debits for net pay, taxes, tips and CalSavers |
| Company -> Chase | monthly statement payment from checking, sometimes partial |
| Company -> landlord, utilities, trash | monthly ACH or autopay |
| Company -> ingredient and packaging suppliers | card charges, ACH, or checks on net-30 terms |
| Company -> bond platform, lenders | monthly and weekly ACH pulls |
| Company -> tax agencies | quarterly sales tax and excise payments |
| Founders -> company | small personal loans when cash is short, repaid later |

## 6. Stories

- **The long slide in cash (all year).** Cash starts near `$77,000`† and ends the year near `$7,000`†. Theo pays the landlord and payroll first, pays the Chase card partially some months, and lets supplier invoices age. By December the accounts payable reach about `$51,000`† and the card balance about `$57,000`†.
- **A dumped batch (spring).** A batch of IPA picks up an infection in the fermenter; Rafa dumps it, losing about `$19,500` of ingredients and work†. The brewery re-orders hops and malt on Dana's card and runs a short of its best-selling beer to its wholesale accounts for three weeks.
- **The card gets reissued, twice.** A fraudulent charge on Dana's Chase card leads Chase to close it and issue a new number; the hop supplier's stored card declines and it emails about a past-due invoice. Later another card is closed after a lost wallet. Charges and credits are split across old and new card numbers.
- **The account nobody should write checks on.** A new bookkeeper pays a supplier with a check from the wrong checkbook; it clears against `DO NOT USE FOR CHECKS ••7092`†, overdraws it, and Bank of America charges an overdraft fee before Theo moves money over.
- **Wholesale customers owe more and more.** The bottle-shop chain stretches from 45 to 90 days, a restaurant closes owing two invoices and keeps four kegs, and receivables grow from about `$17,000`† to about `$86,000`† by year end. Marcus collects a pile of checks at once in December.
- **Holiday events and gift cards (November-December).** Companies book holiday parties with card deposits in October and November and pay balances in December; one pays by ACH in January. Gift card sales spike before Christmas, leaving about `$24,000` outstanding† to be redeemed in the new year.
- **Tips and the drawer.** Card tips are paid through every payroll run; cash tips are handed out at close. The drawer runs over or short most weeks, about `$4,100` short over the year†.
- **A founder lends the payroll.** Before a payroll in August, Theo moves money from his personal account to checking and records it as a loan; he repays himself in part in December.
- **Monthly bond and loan pulls.** The bond platform pulls its payment and `$25` fee every month†; once it hits an empty account and is retried two days later.
- **Annual and quarterly bills.** The insurance premium is financed in monthly installments; the hop contract's annual prepayment lands in the fall; quarterly sales tax and excise payments come due.

## 7. Kinds of events

Over the year, its records show these kinds of events:

- **Taproom:** daily card sales with tips, service charges and surcharges; comps and discounts; cash deposits that lag the day; cash over and short; gift cards sold and redeemed; event deposits and balances.
- **Wholesale:** invoices on different terms, checks collected at delivery, slow payers, a customer that closes owing money, keg deposits charged and refunded, kegs never returned.
- **Purchases:** ingredients, packaging, storage, equipment rental, a dumped batch replaced, an annual hop contract.
- **People:** semi-monthly payroll with tips; a founder's personal loan to the company and its partial repayment; guaranteed payments skipped when cash is short.
- **Institutions:** next-day Square payouts net of fees; Shopify payouts; Chase cards closed and reissued; partial card payments; an overdraft and its fee; bond and loan pulls, one retried; checks clearing out of order.
- **Taxes and deposits:** quarterly sales tax, beer excise, container deposits collected and remitted.
