# Ridgeline Stays: a vacation-rental manager holding other people's money

This describes one business, its past and its year. Facts marked † come from public sources on how vacation-rental money moves: Vacasa's annual reports to the SEC and Airbnb's published payout rules (see `SOURCES.md`); a † vouches for the fact it follows, not for the date or reason the story gives it. No single small company stands behind this business: its size and people are invented.

## 1. The business and the time frame

- **Business:** Ridgeline Stays LLC, a vacation-rental management company in Pigeon Forge, Tennessee, at the edge of the Smoky Mountains. It manages 42 cabins owned by other people: it lists them on Airbnb, Vrbo and its own website, sets prices, handles guests, sends cleaners and repair people between stays, and pays each owner what is left after its commission and costs.
- **The money is mostly not its own.** Guests pay before they stay, sometimes months before. That money belongs partly to the owner, partly to the tax authorities and partly to Ridgeline, and Ridgeline earns it only when the stay happens†. Until then it sits in a separate trust account†, and owners are paid their share monthly after their guests' stays†.
- **Size:** about `$3.4 million` of bookings a year across the 42 cabins. Ridgeline keeps a 25% commission on rent plus the cleaning fees it charges guests, and pays cleaners per turnover. It has six office employees and about thirty cleaners and handymen working as contractors.
- **Time range:** 2023-01-01 to 2023-12-31, currency USD. Busy in summer, peak in October (fall colors) and at Christmas, slow in January and February.

## 2. History before the start

- Founded in 2015 by a couple who managed their own two cabins and then their neighbors'. Most owners live elsewhere (Atlanta, Nashville, Ohio) and bought cabins as investments in 2020-2021.
- On 2023-01-01 the trust account holds about `$610,000` of guest prepayments for stays from January to October, plus the owners' December earnings due on January 10. December's lodging taxes are due in January. Airbnb payouts for New Year's Eve check-ins are in transit. Several December repairs have not been charged to owners yet. A guest's damage claim from a Christmas stay is open with Airbnb.

## 3. Institutions (the only sources of data)

- **Smoky Valley Bank:** three accounts. The trust account (`Trust ••6604`) receives every guest payment and pays owners, refunds, taxes and the owners' expenses; the operating account (`Operating ••6611`) receives Ridgeline's commission and fees, moved from trust once each month after the stays, and pays payroll, rent and overhead; a tax account (`Tax ••6629`) holds lodging taxes until they are filed. Posted transactions and monthly statements.
- **Airbnb:** each reservation's payout is released about 24 hours after the guest's scheduled check-in†, and a bank transfer arrives 3-5 business days later†. Stays of 28 nights or more are paid in monthly installments†. Guest refunds the host agrees to, and money Airbnb takes back for cancellations, come out of later payouts. Damage claims are paid separately after review.
- **Vrbo:** pays out after check-in on its own schedule, with its own fees and descriptors.
- **Stripe (direct bookings):** guests on Ridgeline's site pay a 50% deposit at booking and the balance 30 days before arrival; a refundable damage deposit is held as a card authorization and only charged if there is damage. Payouts to the trust account every business day, net of fees.
- **Payroll provider:** biweekly payroll for six office employees.
- **Business card:** supplies (linens, toiletries, light bulbs, hot tub chemicals) bought at warehouse and hardware stores, paid monthly from operating; items for a specific cabin are charged back to that cabin's owner.
- **Tax agencies:** monthly state sales tax and local occupancy tax returns for the bookings Ridgeline collects tax on itself.

## 4. The cast

- **Carla and Jim Pruitt, owners of Ridgeline.** Carla runs owners and money: she sends the monthly owner statements and approves every payout. Jim runs operations: cleaners, repairs and the 2 a.m. phone calls about hot tubs.
- **Office staff (six):** two reservation agents, a guest-services lead, an operations coordinator who schedules turnovers, a bookkeeper who reconciles the trust account each month, and an owner-relations manager.
- **Cleaners and handymen (about thirty contractors):** paid per turnover or per job every week by ACH from operating. A few are always reliable; one crew no-shows on a busy Saturday in October.
- **Vendors:** a linen service, a hot tub service company, a pest control company, an HVAC contractor, a locksmith, a snow and road-clearing service, the landlord of the office.
- **Owners (42):** each with a management agreement: monthly statement and payout by ACH on the 10th, repairs over `$300` approved in advance, an onboarding fee for new owners. Some read every line; some call when a payout is smaller than they expected; one owner owes Ridgeline money after a slow month with a big repair.
- **Guests:** families, couples and groups booking through Airbnb (most), Vrbo, and directly (repeat guests). Some cancel within the refund window, some ask for refunds after a problem, a few damage things, one traveling nurse books two months.

## 5. Money flows

| Flow | How it moves |
|---|---|
| Airbnb guests -> Airbnb -> trust | per-reservation payouts released about a day after check-in†, landing 3-5 business days later† |
| Vrbo guests -> Vrbo -> trust | payouts after check-in, in batches |
| Direct guests -> Stripe -> trust | deposit at booking, balance 30 days out, damage deposit held and sometimes charged; daily payouts net of fees |
| Trust -> guests | refunds of direct bookings |
| Trust -> owners | monthly payout on the 10th for last month's stays, less commission, cleaning and expenses† |
| Trust -> operating | Ridgeline's commission and fees, once stays have happened† |
| Trust -> tax account -> tax agencies | lodging taxes set aside, then filed monthly |
| Operating -> cleaners, handymen, vendors | weekly ACH for turnovers and jobs; vendor invoices; charged back to owners where they belong |
| Operating -> payroll, office rent, card | biweekly, monthly |
| Owners -> trust | an owner covers a negative balance |

## 6. Stories

- **The ice storm (late January).** A storm closes the mountain roads for three days. Guests cancel; Airbnb refunds them under its extenuating-circumstances rules and takes the money back out of later payouts; Ridgeline refunds its direct guests from trust. January owner payouts are thin, and two owners call.
- **An owner leaves (March) and another joins (May).** One owner sells her cabin. Ridgeline sends a final statement and passes the prepayments it holds for her already-booked summer stays to the buyer's manager. A new owner signs in May: an onboarding fee, new linens, a smart lock and hot tub cover charged to his account leave him owing Ridgeline money until his first bookings pay.
- **The traveling nurse (June-July).** A two-month Airbnb stay pays a first installment a day after check-in and the next a month later†.
- **The hot tub that won't heat (August).** A family's hot tub fails on day one; Jim sends the service company the next morning; the guest asks for a partial refund through Airbnb, which comes out of a later payout. The repair is charged to the owner after he approves it by text.
- **October.** Every cabin is booked every weekend. Airbnb payouts land almost daily; cleaning crews work double shifts; one crew misses a Saturday, so the office staff clean two cabins themselves and a guest checks in two hours late and gets a goodwill refund. The October payouts to owners on November 10 are the biggest of the year.
- **A damage claim (November).** Guests break a TV and stain a sofa. Ridgeline files with Airbnb, which pays part of the claim three weeks later; the owner is credited the payment and charged for the replacement.
- **The trust-account slip (September).** A new bookkeeper pays the office's internet bill from the trust account by mistake. The month-end reconciliation catches it and operating repays trust the next week.
- **Owner statement corrections.** A booking is assigned to the wrong cabin in the property system; two owners' statements are corrected the following month with an adjustment line each.
- **The late tax return.** One month's local occupancy tax return is filed four days late during the October rush, and a penalty notice arrives in December.

## 7. Kinds of events

Over the year, its records show these kinds of events:

- **Guests:** bookings months ahead, deposits and balances, payouts released after check-in and landing days later, long stays paid monthly, cancellations with refunds, refunds taken back from later platform payouts, goodwill refunds, damage deposits held and charged, a damage claim paid weeks later.
- **Owners:** monthly statements and payouts, commission and cleaning deducted, repairs charged back, an owner who leaves and one who joins, a negative owner balance, statement corrections.
- **Trust money:** guest funds held until stays, the manager's share moved to operating only after stays, taxes set aside and filed monthly, a payment from the wrong account and its repayment, a late filing penalty.
- **Operations:** weekly contractor payments per turnover, vendor invoices, supplies on the card, office payroll, a crew that misses a day.
- **Season:** a storm that cancels a week, a slow winter, a summer and October peak, Christmas.
