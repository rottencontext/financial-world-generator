# Sources: Ridgeline Stays

Unlike the other folders, this world is not modeled on one small company: I found no small vacation-rental manager with public financial statements. Its mechanics come from the annual reports of Vacasa, the largest US vacation-rental manager, filed with the SEC, and from Airbnb's published payout rules. Its size, location, people and events are invented.

The phrases "funds payable to owners", "amounts held on behalf of customers", "rescission period" and "bankruptcy-remote" were confirmed in all four 10-Ks with EDGAR full-text search. The monthly payout wording was matched by search terms, not read in full.

## Platform rules

- Airbnb releases a host's payout about 24 hours after the guest's scheduled check-in, bank transfers take 3-5 business days, and stays of 28 nights or more are paid in monthly installments: [Airbnb help: when you'll get your payout](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3133), [Airbnb resources for hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/20).

## Invented for this world

The company, its location, its 42 cabins, every person, the bank and account numbers, the 25% commission, the payout day, Vrbo's and Stripe's exact timing, which lodging taxes each platform collects, and every event in section 6. Tennessee's trust-account rules for vacation-rental managers were not checked; the world keeps a separate trust account because the 10-Ks say some states require it.

## Finding a small company to anchor it

EDGAR full-text search for Form C filings mentioning Airbnb returns mostly single-property developments raising money to build (for example a planned cabin retreat with no revenue yet). Regulation A filings for small rental funds, or a state real estate commission's list of licensed vacation-lodging firms, are the next places to look.
