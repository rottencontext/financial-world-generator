# Sources: Bayview Identity

Modeled on **Intellicheck, Inc.** (Nasdaq: IDN, CIK 1040896), an identity verification software company in Melville, New York. The facts come from its amended 10-K for 2021. The world keeps the real company's product, pricing model, customer types and concentration, revenue, headcount by function, the nature of the restatement (option awards that should have been liability awards from Q3 2020; shares surrendered on exercise that were not sold, with the equivalent taxes not remitted), the restated expense and net loss, the material weakness, and the filing dates. The names, people, bank, systems, audit firm, the way the problem was discovered, the customer dispute and the tax notices are invented.

Grid cell: **record-to-report, tier 3** (90 days; an audit world whose filed report must be restated). It differs from `data_security_saas_audit_90day.md`, whose audit is about weekend entries, round-dollar adjustments and request lists. Here the hard part is technical accounting and payroll tax.

## Inferred

- That the withholding showed in payroll registers as withheld but was never deposited. The filing says only that surrendered shares were not sold and the funds not remitted. The world's version is one plausible way that happens.

## Invented for this world

The names (Bayview Identity Systems, Sound Shore Bank, Stone & Lasker LLP), every person, NetSuite and the other systems, the payroll provider's reconciliation as the trigger, the number of exercises and employees involved, the customer's disputed invoice, the tax notices, corrected W-2s, and the window's dated events other than the two filing dates. The filing does not say how the error was found.

## Known differences from the filing

- The filing's pages read here do not give the pre-restatement figures, so the original (wrong) net loss in the world is the restated loss less the added expense (for 2021, about $4,147K).
