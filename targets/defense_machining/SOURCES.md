## Real business email compromise cases behind the story

The filing reports no fraud loss. The heat treater story is invented, but its shape follows losses that public companies have disclosed:

- NETSTREIT (Q2 2024): two fraudulent transfers totaling $3.3 million to someone impersonating a development partner, $2.8 million lost after insurance: [earnings release, July 2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1798100/000179810024000064/netstreitearningsrelease-q.htm).
- Journey Medical (Q3 2021): about $9.5 million "erroneously wired to fraudulent accounts" in a business email compromise: [earnings release, December 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1867066/000110465921148566/tm2135030d1_ex99-1.htm).

## Inferred

- **When Stadco was bought.** The prompt says 2021, from the Stadco term loan's 2028 maturity and the 10-K's ongoing purchase-accounting weakness. The filing pages read here do not state the date.
- **Sales between the two plants.** The two segments' revenue sums to $34,163K, while revenue by industry sums to $34,031K. The $132K gap is likely sales between Ranor and Stadco eliminated in consolidation. That is the basis for the intercompany story.
- **Purchase volume.** About 650 invoices a quarter and 210 active vendors are estimates for a $30 million cost base with customer-supplied metal.

## Known differences from the filing

- **Segment gross results.** The summaries read of the segment table give gross results that do not exactly equal revenue minus cost of revenue. The prompt avoids exact segment gross figures and uses revenue and operating results only.
- **Largest customer's share.** The filing gives it as 23% in one place and 22% in the risk factors. The prompt uses 23%.
- **Auditor change.** The 10-K was filed late, after a Nasdaq notice, and the auditor changed from Marcum (FY2024) to CBIZ (FY2025). Neither is used in the world, which ends on March 31, 2025.
