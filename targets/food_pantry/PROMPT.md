# Cedar Bend Community Food Pantry: a fast-growing nonprofit that builds its own building

This describes one organization, its past and the period it lives through. Facts marked † come from the real pantry's public IRS Form 990 filings and local news coverage (see `SOURCES.md`); a † vouches for the fact it follows, not for the date or reason the story gives it. The pantry, its people and its account numbers are invented.

## 1. The organization and the time frame

- **Organization:** Cedar Bend Community Food Pantry, a 501(c)(3) food pantry in a suburb of an eastern Iowa university town, tax-exempt since 2016†. Families shop for free in a client-choice pantry several days a week. It has a handful of paid staff and hundreds of volunteers.
- **Growth:** revenue went from about `$683,000` in 2018† to `$1,498,000` in 2021†, `$1,850,000` in 2022† and `$2,455,000` in 2023†. Nearly all of it, 97-99%†, is contributions: individual gifts, churches, businesses, foundations, workplace giving and donated food. Fundraising events net about `$22,000` in 2022† and `$67,000` in 2023†.
- **Spending:** `$1,308,000` in 2022† and `$2,238,000` in 2023†, most of it food: bought from the regional food bank and from wholesalers, and donated food passed straight to families. Payroll is small: the executive director earns about `$61,000` (2022)† to `$66,000` (2023)†, other staff together about `$111,000`† to `$150,000`†, plus payroll taxes of about `$13,000`† to `$17,000`†.
- **Time range:** 2022-01-01 to 2023-06-30 (18 months), currency USD. It spans the building project from groundbreaking to move-in.

## 2. History before the start

- Founded by volunteers in 2016†, the pantry spent five years in a former post office building it outgrew†. Demand rose through the pandemic.
- A neighboring church donated land on its lot for a new building†. The board launched a capital campaign with a `$1.2 million` goal† for a 5,000 sq ft pantry, more than twice the old space†. By the start of 2022 roughly `$700,000` is raised† in cash and multi-year pledges.
- On 2022-01-01 total assets are about `$987,000`† and liabilities almost nothing (`$7,212`†). December's year-end gifts are still arriving: checks dated December 31 come in the mail through the first two weeks of January. The online giving platform's last payout of 2021 is in transit. The regional food bank's December invoice is unpaid. Pledges from the campaign are due on donors' own schedules over the next three years.

## 3. Institutions (the only sources of data)

- **Hawkeye Valley Credit Union:** operating checking (`••0417`), a money-market savings account (`••0418`) for reserves, and a building-fund checking account (`••0419`) for the campaign. Posted transactions with running balances and monthly statements. Checks are deposited by remote capture in batches; a donor's check sometimes bounces and comes back with a fee.
- **Credit union line of credit:** a bridge line opened in 2022 (`••0420`) to pay the builder while pledges are still coming in; monthly interest, draws and paydowns.
- **Online giving platform:** one-time and monthly recurring gifts by card and bank transfer, with platform and processing fees, paid out to checking weekly. Donors may add a tip for the platform.
- **PayPal Giving Fund:** gifts made through PayPal and social-media fundraisers, granted to the pantry in one monthly payment with a list of donors.
- **Brokerage account:** receives gifts of stock, which are sold the same or next day and the cash swept to the credit union.
- **Payroll provider:** biweekly payroll for five or six staff.
- **Card:** a credit-union business card for supplies, small food purchases and the walk-in cooler repair, paid monthly.

## 4. The cast

- **Ruth Albrecht, executive director.** The founder, once a volunteer, now the only full-time manager. Writes the thank-you notes herself, keeps the campaign spreadsheet of every pledge, and orders food.
- **Sam Whitcomb, operations manager.** Runs the warehouse, deliveries and the food bank orders; uses the business card.
- **Ana Lucero, volunteer coordinator** (part-time), and **two part-time drivers and warehouse staff**.
- **Board treasurer (a volunteer, retired banker).** Signs checks; any check over `$5,000` needs a second board signature, so builder payments wait for both signers.
- **Donors:** hundreds of individuals giving small amounts online or by check, a few dozen giving monthly; churches sending offering collections; local businesses and a grocery chain giving cash and food; family foundations and the community foundation giving grants; donors giving through donor-advised funds, whose checks name the fund, not the donor; employees giving through workplace campaigns, paid out quarterly; companies matching employees' gifts months later.
- **Capital campaign donors:** pledges paid in installments by check, a few by stock, one foundation challenge grant paid when the pantry raises the match.
- **Builder and architect:** a general contractor paid by monthly applications for payment with 5% retainage, released after completion and the punch list; an architect paid monthly fees.
- **Food suppliers:** the regional food bank (an invoice twice a month for purchased food and handling fees), a wholesale food distributor (dairy, eggs and meat on 14-day terms), local farmers paid by check for summer produce, and grocery-store gift cards bought in bulk for families at the holidays.
- **Others:** the utility (bills rise sharply once the new building's walk-in coolers run), a refrigeration repair company, insurance, a part-time bookkeeper, and volunteers reimbursed for mileage.

## 6. Stories

- **Groundbreaking and building (April 2022-January 2023).** The groundbreaking is on April 14, 2022†. The general contractor bills monthly; each payment waits for two signatures and goes out a week after approval, less 5% retainage. In the summer, steel and cooler prices rise and the contractor submits a change order. Pledge payments lag, so the board opens a bridge line at the credit union and draws on it from August. At the end of 2022 the pantry owes about `$578,000`†, mostly the line of credit and the builder's final bills and retainage. Volunteers stock the new building in late January 2023† and it opens the next week.
- **Paying it down (2023).** The final pay application and the retainage are paid in March after the punch list. Pledge installments, a foundation's challenge grant and a stock gift pay the line down; liabilities fall to about `$201,000`† by the end of 2023 (beyond this world's window).
- **Grocery prices and the line out the door (2022).** Food inflation brings more families each month. Ruth orders more from the food bank and the distributor, and buys more protein than ever; spending grows 31% in 2022† and 71% in 2023†.
- **December (both years).** Most of the year's individual giving arrives in December: online gifts spike on Giving Tuesday, when a local business matches every gift up to `$25,000`, and again on December 30-31. Checks dated December 31 arrive through mid-January. Ruth buys grocery gift cards in bulk for families before Christmas.
- **A basketball star asks fans to give.** A star player from the nearby university's women's team posts a video asking fans to donate to the pantry†; online gifts jump for a week, mostly small amounts from new donors in other states.
- **The spring fundraiser (May 2023).** A dinner and auction with business sponsorships paid by check, tickets sold online, auction items paid by card on the night, and catering and venue costs paid after; it nets about `$67,000`† over the year.
- **A bounced pledge check (fall 2022).** A pledge installment bounces; the credit union reverses the deposit and charges a fee; the donor sends a new check two weeks later.
- **The cooler breaks (June 2023).** A compressor on the new walk-in cooler fails in a heat wave; the repair goes on the business card and the food bank sends an emergency delivery.

## 7. Kinds of events

Over the 18 months, its records show these kinds of events:

- **Giving:** one-time and recurring online gifts net of fees, weekly platform payouts, monthly PayPal Giving Fund grants, checks deposited in batches, a bounced check, donor-advised-fund grants naming the fund, foundation grants, quarterly workplace giving, matching gifts months later, a matching day, a December surge, year-end checks arriving in January, a viral spike of small gifts.
- **Capital campaign:** pledge installments, a stock gift sold and swept, a challenge grant released on a match, a bridge line drawn and paid down.
- **Construction:** monthly pay applications less retainage, a change order, two-signature delays, retainage released after the punch list, architect fees.
- **Food:** food bank invoices twice a month, distributor purchases on 14-day terms, farmers paid by check, bulk gift cards for families.
- **People:** biweekly payroll for a small staff, volunteer mileage reimbursements.
- **Operations:** utility bills that jump in the new building, an emergency repair on the card, insurance, a part-time bookkeeper.
- **Institutions:** remote-capture deposits, returned-item fees, monthly statements, line-of-credit interest, brokerage sweeps.
