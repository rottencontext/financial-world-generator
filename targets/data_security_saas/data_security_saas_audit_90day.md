# Triangle Shield Software: the year-end close and the audit (first quarter of 2023)

This describes one business, its past and ninety days. Facts marked † come from the real company's public filings that this business is modeled on (see `SOURCES.md`). A mark vouches for the fact it follows, not for the date or reason the story gives it. The company, its people and its account numbers are invented.

This is the audit world of the record-to-report row. The company closes 2022, an outside audit firm audits it, and the annual report is filed. The firm asks for documents (a "provided by client" list) and chases what doesn't come. It confirms balances with banks, customers and lenders, tests journal entries and looks for unrecorded liabilities. All the while, one person posts and approves every entry, often on weekends, sometimes in round numbers with no support. Some errors are caught and corrected. One goes into the filed report and is found only months later.

## 1. The business and the time frame

- **Business:** Triangle Shield Software, Inc., Research Triangle Park, North Carolina†. Data security and privacy software†:
  - **VaultRoom:** secure content sharing.
  - **ArchiveCrate:** archiving.
  - **DataCourier:** data placement.
  - **TagSense:** classification.
  - **RewindGuard:** ransomware recovery, bought in January 2022.
  - **PrivacyKit:** WordPress privacy plugins†.
- **How it sells:** Subscriptions, often prepaid for several years and auto-renewing†, recognized evenly over the term†. Some older customers have perpetual licenses with yearly maintenance†. It sells directly, through resellers and distributors† and through the Azure and AWS marketplaces†. It has more than 10,000 customers†.
- **The year being audited (2022):**
  - **Revenue and loss.** Revenue `$2,627,123` (2021: `$3,609,494`)†. Net loss `$9,713,467`†.
  - **Balances at December 31, 2022.** Cash `$1,712`†. Trade receivables `$31,978`†. Deferred revenue `$1,704,249` (2021: `$1,035,185`)†. Accrued liabilities `$554,076`†.
  - **Liquidity.** Current liabilities `$8,604,066`† and a working capital deficit of `$5,752,984`†.
- **After year end:** first-quarter 2023 revenue is `$1,379,806`†. Deferred revenue is `$1,584,809` and accrued liabilities `$553,367` at March 31, 2023†.
- **Staff:** 22 employees and 5 contractors at January 31, 2023†.
- **Time range:** 2023-01-01 to 2023-03-31, ninety days, currency USD. The window covers:
  - the December and year-end close;
  - audit fieldwork in late January and February;
  - the annual report filed on February 24†;
  - the January, February and March closes;
  - New Year's Day (observed Monday, January 2), Martin Luther King Jr. Day and Presidents' Day.

## 2. History before the start

- **Acquisitions.** Growth came by buying small products. The ransomware product was bought in January 2022 for `$250,000` in cash, a `$2.9 million` promissory note and a `$250,000` contingent payment†. A five-year office lease began in July 2022†.
- **Debt.** Financing comes from convertible notes from small-cap lenders, promissory notes and the CEO†, secured by all assets†. During 2022 the company:
  - raised `$2,027,570` of convertible debt and `$3,458,247` of notes†;
  - repaid `$771,718` of convertible notes and `$4,408,240` of notes†;
  - borrowed `$299,281` from the CEO and repaid him `$602,237`†;
  - had `$167,653` of its expenses paid by the CEO†.

  At year end, `$112,064` is due to related parties†. New convertible notes are signed on January 4 and January 24, 2023†.
- **Controls.** These are the weaknesses the annual report will describe†:
  - no controls to check that underlying data is complete and accurate;
  - no written control policies;
  - not enough segregation of duties in accounting;
  - no functioning audit committee or outside directors.
- **On 2023-01-01:**
  - December is open. The deferred revenue schedule was last rolled forward at September 30, 2022.
  - The note schedule doesn't agree with the lenders' statements for two notes.
  - The contingent payment for the ransomware product has not been reassessed.
  - The cash flow worksheet for 2022 has not been started.

## 3. Institutions (the only sources of data)

Each keeps its own records the way it really would. Requests, confirmations, contracts, statements and work papers are records too: one row per document, with the fields on it.

- **Piedmont Ridge Bank:** operating account `••5180`. Posted transactions, monthly statements, and a bank confirmation sent directly to the auditors.
- **QuickBooks Online (the books):** journal entries carry the user who posted them, the date and time posted, and the date they are booked to. Entries can be posted into closed periods. There is no approval step.
- **The close spreadsheets:** deferred revenue by contract, prepaids, the note schedule, accruals and the cash flow worksheet, saved as dated files.
- **The CRM:** contracts with terms, prices, billing schedules and start dates.
- **AWS and Microsoft marketplaces, the plugin store, resellers:** reports, payouts and remittances as in the company's other worlds.
- **Lenders and the transfer agent:**
  - lenders' statements and conversion notices;
  - confirmations of balances and terms, sent directly to the auditors;
  - the transfer agent's share register.
- **Hartwell & Coe LLP, the outside audit firm:**
  - its request list, one row per item, with due date, status and comments;
  - its emails;
  - confirmation requests and replies;
  - a list of proposed adjustments, which management accepts or waives;
  - its sign-off date.
- **Payroll provider:** registers per run, quarterly and annual tax forms.
- **The SEC's EDGAR system:** filings and their dates.

## 4. The cast

- **Jordan Reese, founder and CEO.** Lends to the company, pays some company bills personally, signs the annual report and the management letter. He also serves as the board, with no outside directors.
- **Priya Natarajan, CFO (part-time).** Posts and approves every journal entry. Builds every schedule and answers every audit request, mostly late at night and on weekends.
- **Ben Carter, staff accountant (joins in May; in this window, an outside bookkeeper does his work).** Enters bills, payouts and bank matches.
- **Hartwell & Coe's engagement team.** A partner who appears twice, a manager who chases the request list, and a senior who tests journal entries and sends confirmations.
- **Lenders.** Five noteholders and the seller of the ransomware product. Two are slow to return confirmations.
- **Customers.** A few large prepaid customers selected for confirmation. One replies with a different contract start date.

## 6. Stories

- **The December close (January 2-20).** Priya rolls the deferred revenue schedule forward from September 30 to December 31. Its total must come to `$1,704,249`†. Three multi-year deals signed in December are billed in full. The schedule spreads one of them over three years, but the books release a year of it in December. She accrues December wages, the December contractor invoices and the audit fee. She posts eighteen entries on Sunday, January 15 between 9 p.m. and midnight, all booked to December 31.
- **The request list (January 16-February 17).** The audit firm's list has 64 items:
  - bank statements and reconciliations;
  - the deferred revenue rollforward with contracts for a sample;
  - the note schedule with every agreement, amendment and conversion notice;
  - the purchase agreement and contingent payment for the ransomware product;
  - payroll registers;
  - related-party balances;
  - the lease;
  - subsequent receipts and payments;
  - the cash flow worksheet.

  Priya delivers about half on time. The manager sends three reminder emails. The note agreements arrive in pieces, and two amendments are missing until February 13.
- **Confirmations (January 23-February 17).** The bank confirms `$1,712`. Two lenders confirm balances that differ from the note schedule by unbooked accrued interest and by a conversion booked in the wrong month. One large customer confirms a contract that started on February 1, 2022, not January 1, which moves a month of revenue. The seller of the ransomware product confirms the note.
- **Journal entry testing (February).** The senior pulls every entry posted after December 31 and booked to 2022, and every entry with a round amount. She finds:
  - a `$50,000` entry "reclass to deferred revenue" posted on a Sunday, with no support;
  - a `$30,000` entry moving contractor costs to prepaid;
  - entries posted and approved by the same person.

  Priya supports the first with a spreadsheet tab made the day before. She reverses the second when asked.
- **Unrecorded liabilities (February).** The senior reviews January and February payments and finds:
  - `$18,400` of December legal work invoiced in January;
  - a hosting invoice for December;
  - a December software renewal the CEO paid on his own card and expensed in January.

  The firm proposes adjusting entries. Management accepts two and waives one as immaterial.
- **Going concern and new notes (February).** With `$1,712` of cash and a working capital deficit of `$5,752,984`†, the firm asks for a cash forecast. The January 4 and January 24 convertible notes† and the CEO's loans are disclosed as events after year end.
- **The filing (February 24).** The annual report is filed on February 24†. It describes the four control weaknesses† and reports operating cash used of `$2,886,337` and financing cash of `$2,244,244`†. Certain convertible-note balances are shown as financing cash flows when they belong in operating†. Nobody notices until the second-quarter report in August, when an amended report changes them to `$1,252,650` and `$610,557`†.
- **The first quarter of 2023 (March).** A large deal with a term license is recognized partly at once and partly over time. Revenue for the quarter is `$1,379,806`† and deferred revenue falls to `$1,584,809`†. Priya closes January and February late, with the same Sunday-night pattern.

## 7. Kinds of events

Over the ninety days, its records show these kinds of events:

- **Close:** deferred revenue rolled forward over a quarter, accruals for wages, contractors and fees, entries posted late at night and on weekends, entries booked to a closed year, round-dollar adjustments, one person posting and approving.
- **Audit:** a request list with due dates and reminders, documents delivered late or in pieces, bank, customer and lender confirmations with differences, journal entry testing, a search for unrecorded liabilities, proposed adjustments accepted and waived, a going-concern review, events after year end.
- **Debt and equity:** new convertible notes, conversions, accrued interest, a promissory note for an acquisition, a contingent payment, loans from the CEO.
- **Reporting:** an annual report filed with a statement of cash flows that misclassifies note balances, discovered months later.

## 8. What the world must also remember

So that an agent working these records can be graded, the world keeps, in its own state and not in any institution's records, the truth behind every record:

- the correct December 31, 2022 balances for deferred revenue, accruals, notes, related-party loans and the contingent payment;
- every adjustment the auditors proposed, and which were booked;
- which journal entries were unsupported and what they changed;
- the liabilities still unrecorded after the audit;
- the correct classification of every 2022 cash flow, including the convertible-note balances the filed report got wrong.
